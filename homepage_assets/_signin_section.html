How to use these toolsA short walkthrough — start here if it’s your first run

Everything runs inside your own browser. Your leases, ledgers and rent rolls are never uploaded anywhere — close the tab and they’re gone.

1

Pull the right reports out of ResMan

You need the Rent Roll — the one that lists each unit with its own Description/Amount charge breakdown underneath. ResMan also offers a Rent Roll Summary; that one only has property-level totals and there’s nothing in it to check, so the tools will stop and ask you for the full report if you grab it by mistake.

Then, depending on the tool: the resident documents export (leases), a resident ledger, or your LeaseLock invoice.

2

Set it up before you upload

Charge Mappings teach the tool your property’s naming, so “Valet Trash” and “Trash Fee” are understood as the same charge. Option Filters let you switch off anything you don’t want flagged — every check and every charge type is listed, including move-in specials.

Anything you switch off is never deleted. It still shows in that unit’s detail, greyed out and labelled, and the true total always appears above your results — so you can hide noise without ever losing sight of what was found.

3

Upload

Click Choose lease files and pick whether you’re uploading individual PDFs or one ZIP. If you use the ZIP, drop in the whole ResMan documents export — each unit’s most recently signed lease is found automatically, and everything else in the ZIP is skipped.

Where several leases exist for one unit, the one actually in effect today is used — not just the newest file. If it genuinely can’t tell, the unit is marked Needs review rather than guessed at.

4

Read the results

Use the tabs to move between everything, discrepancies only, and clean matches. Click any column — unit, lease start or end, deposit — to sort by it. Click a row to open that unit and see every charge and check side by side with the reasoning.

Tick Show only the mismatching lines to collapse the whole property into a single flat list of just what doesn’t match.

5

Export

CSV or Excel to work the list, or PDF for a clean, branded report you can send to an owner or attach to an audit file. The PDF lists every discrepancy grouped by unit, and states plainly if anything was hidden by your filters.

Green isn’t always a match. Some rows are correct without being identical — a resident past their lease term who is properly billed a month-to-month fee, for example. Those are marked as expected and explained, rather than flagged. Anything the tool can’t justify stays flagged.
